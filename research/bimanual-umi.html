Imitation Learning for Bimanual Robot Coordination

Adapting the Stanford UMI demonstration-to-policy framework for dual RX150 robot arms.

← Back to Research

This project adapts the Stanford UMI (Universal Manipulation Interface) demonstration-to-policy framework to dual Interbotix RX150 robot arms. Two custom UMI-style handheld gripper sets were built to collect human demonstration data, which was used to train a diffusion policy for a bimanual pick-and-place task: handing off a block between the two arms. A hardware translation layer converts the model's predicted UMI-style actions into physical RX150 joint movements.

Implementation and further model training are currently in progress. The RX150 arms will be replaced with new WidowX arms going forward; continuing work includes collecting additional demonstration data on the new hardware, retraining the policy, and validating it on the physical WidowX system.

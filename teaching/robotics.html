Robotics

Manipulator kinematics, velocity control, and learning-based manipulation, taught on the arms the research runs on. Undergraduate and graduate sections meet together and are assessed apart.

The course

Robotics is a subject where the gap between a correct derivation and a working arm is wider than it looks. A transformation chain that type-checks on paper can still send an end effector into the table. This course is built so that every result a student derives is then run on hardware, and the hardware is the arbiter.

The sequence starts with frames and transformations, builds forward kinematics twice by two independent methods so that each checks the other, moves through the Jacobian into velocity and force control, and ends on a six-axis arm doing work that cannot be solved in closed form. Graduate students take the same projects with an additional analytical component.

Projects

Three projects, each on real hardware. Students work at an assigned station in the robotics lab and submit through GitHub.

01

Kinematics of the RX150

Assign frames to a real five-axis manipulator, derive its DH table, implement forward kinematics from that table, and verify it three independent ways: against the product of exponentials, against the vendor stack, and against the arm itself with a ruler.

Project brief ReactorX 150
02

Jacobian and velocity control

Build the manipulator Jacobian, drive the end effector along a commanded Cartesian velocity, and find out what happens near a singularity when the inverse stops being well conditioned.

ReactorX 150
03

Six-axis manipulation on the WidowX AI

Move to a six-degree-of-freedom research arm, where the extra axis buys full orientation control and costs a closed-form solution. Numerical inverse kinematics, full pose targets, and a task that needs both.

How the course is run

One station per team

Teams of three share an assigned station with its own arm and GPU workstation. Analysis and code are individual work; hardware measurement is done together, because measuring an arm is a job for more than one person.

Everything in version control

Each team works in a private repository in the lab's GitHub organisation. Code, figures, and the written report all live there, and the commit history is part of what is assessed.

Nothing to install

Every station runs the same environment: ROS 2, the vendor stacks, and the Python libraries, all pre-installed and identical across all ten machines. Students open a terminal and start working.

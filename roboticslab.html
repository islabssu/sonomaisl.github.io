SSU Robotics Lab

A ten-station robot learning facility at Sonoma State University. Every station pairs a manipulator with a GPU workstation, so a student can record demonstrations, train a policy, and run it on the arm without leaving the bench.

What the lab is for

The lab exists to close the loop between learning and hardware. Policy work that stops at simulation does not tell you whether a method survives contact, latency, and a gripper that does not quite close where the model thought it would. Every station here is configured so that the same machine collects the data, trains the model, and drives the arm.

Two tiers of hardware serve two purposes. The ReactorX 150 arms are the teaching platform: low payload, low consequence, and forgiving of the mistakes a student makes while learning to assign frames. The WidowX AI arms are the research platform: six degrees of freedom, a metre of reach, and the torque to do work that matters. A student who learns kinematics on an RX150 in the fall is running imitation learning on a WidowX AI by spring without changing laboratories.

Software

A single shared environment on every machine, so nothing depends on which station a student happened to sit at.

CapabilityStack
Robot middleware ROS 2 Jazzy Jalisco, RViz, the Interbotix X-Series ROS 2 stack, and URDF models that match the hardware
Arm control Trossen Arm Python SDK for the WidowX AI, interbotix_xs drivers for the RX150, joint and Cartesian control on both
Deep learning PyTorch with CUDA on Blackwell, scikit-learn, and the full NumPy and SciPy stack
Kinematics modern_robotics for screw-theory and product-of-exponentials work, alongside the analytic solvers in the vendor stacks
Simulation MuJoCo for contact-rich policy training, Gazebo Harmonic for ROS 2 integration
Perception OpenCV, Open3D, AprilTag detection, and the native RealSense and DepthAI Python bindings
Photoreal simulation NVIDIA Isaac Sim and Isaac Lab, for large-scale parallel environments and sim-to-real transfer on the same GPUs that run the policies
Why one shared environment. Every machine is built from one disk image and runs one Python environment that every account shares. A student does not install anything, a result that reproduces at one station reproduces at all ten, and the GPU libraries are pinned once rather than negotiated thirty times.

Who uses it

Courses

Undergraduate and graduate

The lab is the teaching space for robotics at Sonoma State. Students work on the same hardware the research runs on, not a scaled-down teaching version of it.

Research

Undergraduate-led

Projects are run by undergraduates with graduate students alongside them, which is unusual and deliberate.
